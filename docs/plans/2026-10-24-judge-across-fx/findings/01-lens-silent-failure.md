That part is correct. The printed rows do not show the count, though. `scoreRow` and `ruleRow` omit `FeatureScore.unavailable`, which `scoreFeature` computes at `judge-score.ts:47`. A human reading the stdout row, which task 02 copies into scores.md, sees only `confident n/total`. For precision features an unavailable item is indistinguishable from an `unsure` one. A run with the judge down or timing out can print a plausible row, such as 0% with `confident 0/N`, and log-only. The only trace is the per-item stderr lines, which are not copied. This breaks the rule that a judge failure must be visible in the counts. It is also the main fault in the "unavailable scored as unsure and counted" path.

[Important] `/development/fx/.worktrees/judge-across-fx/src/scripts/judge-calibrate.ts:192-193`: in the concurrency loop, `(await ask()).latencyMs` is pushed into `sequential` without checking `result.ok`, and sequential failures are never counted. The `failures` counter covers the concurrent calls only. If the judge errors quickly, the "sequential median" in `concurrencyRow` is the latency of failed calls and is reported as a real number.
